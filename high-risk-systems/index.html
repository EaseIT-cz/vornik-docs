    Building a high-risk system on Vornik¶

    Last updated: 15 November 2026

    This page is for you if you put Vornik to a use listed in Annex III of the EU AI Act: for example, filtering job applications or evaluating creditworthiness. Vornik is not placed on the market as a high-risk system. Give it such a purpose and the AI Act makes you the provider of that high-risk system (Article 25(1)(c)). You then take on the provider's duties under Article 16, including the instructions for use of Article 13. For Annex III systems these apply from 2 December 2027 (Article 113, point (c)(i), as amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744).

    EaseIT Labs, as the initial provider, owes you cooperation (Article 25(2)): the information, technical documentation, known limitations and failure modes, and technical access you need. This page is that information, in the order of Article 13(3), so you can write your own instructions for use from it. Later sections add the technical-documentation map (Annex IV), serious incidents (Article 73) and data governance (Article 10); human oversight (Article 14) is under point (d). It does not make Vornik a certified high-risk system, and it does not replace your own conformity assessment.

    What Vornik is¶

    Vornik is an agent orchestration platform. It runs AI-agent workflows in isolated containers, calls the models you configure, keeps a memory of past work, and stops for a human where you tell it to. Its intended purpose names no Annex III use. Defining the high-risk purpose, and everything that follows from it, is yours.

    Article 13(3), point by point¶

    (a) The provider¶

    You are the provider of your high-risk system and name yourself in your instructions for use. EaseIT Labs s.r.o. (Czech Republic) is the initial provider of Vornik and is established in the Union, so no authorised representative applies to it. Contact: enterprise@vornik.io (see Support).

    (b)(i) Intended purpose¶

    Vornik's own purpose is orchestrating AI agents. The Annex III purpose is the one you define: the task, the people it affects, and the decisions it informs.

    (b)(ii) Accuracy, robustness and cybersecurity¶

    Vornik states no accuracy for your task. Accuracy depends on the models, prompts, tools and data you choose, which Vornik cannot measure for you. It gives you the means to measure it:

    • Evals: repeatable test suites for a swarm, with a scoreboard and a regression diff between runs.
    • The audit logs described under (f) below, to examine what each run did.
    • The figures Vornik has measured on its own reference tasks, each with what it does not show: Benchmark results. They are not figures for your task.

    Robustness and cybersecurity controls, and what they do not cover, are in the security model, including its residual risks.

    (b)(iii) Circumstances that can lead to risk¶

    The ones that matter most for a decision about a person:

    Circumstance What Vornik does about it What remains
    A model is wrong Workflows can add a review step or an approval before a result is used A model can still be confidently wrong; a reviewer who does not read defeats the step
    Prompt injection from content the agents read Untrusted content is scanned and wrapped; writes can require approval Mitigated, not solved (residual risk 4)
    A tool is misused Each role gets only the tools its job lists; write tools can require approval A tool you allow is a tool an agent can call
    Work runs unattended Autonomy runs only in projects where it is enabled, under per-hour caps and budgets An autonomous project acts without a person in each run

    (b)(iv) Explaining output¶

    Each task keeps its steps, the prompt each step received, and every tool call with its redacted input and output. task_explain (on the companion interface) and the task page show why a task ended as it did: its failure class, each step's outcome and the last error.

    (b)(v) Performance on specific groups of persons¶

    Vornik trains no model and holds no data about groups of persons. Performance on the people your system affects depends on the models you choose and on your prompts and data; measure it with your own test sets (evals above).

    (b)(vi) Input data¶

    Vornik does not train or fine-tune models. It sends the content of each task to the model endpoints you configure. What those models were trained on is their providers' to state.

    (b)(vii) Interpreting output¶

    Conversational replies tell people they come from an AI system. Published output carries a machine-readable mark where the channel can carry one; where it cannot (a Telegram message), the deployment's ledger records it over the exact bytes. A reader checks content against that deployment's own ledger. A mark can be stripped, and an edited copy is not recognised. Details and limits: AI transparency.

    (c) Pre-determined changes¶

    Vornik has had no conformity assessment, so no change is pre-determined in that sense. Releases carry calendar versions; each release's changes, and any action it needs before an upgrade, are in the release notes.

    (d) Human oversight (Article 14)¶

    Article 14(3) leaves the oversight measures to you: you build them into your system, or name them for your deployers. Each capability Article 14(4) asks for, the Vornik control you can use for it, and what that control does not do:

    Article 14(4) Vornik control What it does not do
    (a) understand the system and monitor it, including anomalies the table under (b)(iii); each task's page; vornikctl doctor; the metrics and dashboards; the operator Inbox, which lists failures it shows what ran, not whether a decision about a person was right
    (b) stay aware of automation bias, in particular for recommendations approval steps show the proposed action before it runs; replies say they come from an AI system (AI transparency) nothing stops a reviewer approving without reading: that is your deployers' training and your interface
    (c) interpret the output each task's steps, prompts and tool calls, and task_explain (see (b)(iv)) it explains what the agents did, not why a model answered as it did
    (d) decide not to use, disregard, override or reverse the output reject an approval; a result is a file that changes nothing until a person or an approved write tool uses it; a change to the control plane can be rolled back on the admin console's Proposals tab an action an approved write tool already sent (an email, an order) is not undone by Vornik
    (e) intervene or stop, to a safe state cancel a task on its execution page or with vornikctl task cancel <taskId>: its agent container is stopped and the task marked cancelled. vornikctl task pause pauses one; autonomy.enabled: false stops a project's self-created work (Autonomy) a tool call already in flight may complete; the stop is per task or per project

    For a remote biometric identification system (Annex III point 1(a)), Article 14(5) asks for two people to verify each identification. A Vornik approval step takes one approver, so you build the second verification.

    Your deployers assign oversight to people with the competence, training and authority for it (Article 26(2)); that part is organisational.

    (e) Resources, lifetime and maintenance¶

    Vornik runs on Linux with rootless Podman and PostgreSQL with pgvector (Getting started). It has no fixed end of life. Releases come several times a month (seven in October 2026), and security fixes target the latest release (Security). The podman updater, vornik-update.sh, upgrades in place, takes a database and configuration backup first, and prints the commands to roll back; read each release's upgrade requirements in the release notes before upgrading. Retention of each record type is configurable (Storage and retention).

    (f) Logs (Article 12)¶

    • Tool calls: every tool call an agent makes, with redacted input and output, kept at least 180 days. Only tool_audit_floor_override: true with a stated basis lowers that.
    • Chat and step records: conversation audit, step prompts and step outcomes, scanned for secrets before they are stored (the doctor check redaction_seams warns when that is off; see the vornikctl reference).
    • Exports: vornikctl subject exports what is linked to one person; backups export the rest.

    Retention periods and what each record holds: Storage and retention.

    Serious incidents (Article 73)¶

    The report to the market surveillance authority is yours. Report immediately once you establish a causal link between your system and a serious incident, or its reasonable likelihood; after a death, as soon as you suspect one. Counted from when you, or where applicable your deployer, become aware of the incident, the outer limits are 15 days; two days for a widespread infringement or a serious incident under Article 3, point (49)(b); and 10 days in the event of a death. Article 73(6) also has you investigate without delay, cooperate with the authority, and not alter the system in a way that could affect the evaluation of the causes before you inform the authority. With Vornik, while you investigate from the records:

    1. Take a backup at once: vornikctl backup --out <file> (Storage and retention). It holds every record present when it is taken; tool calls are kept at least 180 days.
    2. Raise the retention periods, so the sweeper deletes nothing after it.
    3. Make no upgrade or configuration change that alters the system until you have informed the authority. An upgrade migrates the database; a configuration change alters workflows and prompts.

    Vornik has no single switch that suspends every deletion; steps 1 and 2 do the same together. Article 73(1) speaks of systems "placed on the Union market"; if you only put your system into service for your own use, plan for Article 73 anyway: your deployers must tell you and the authority of a serious incident either way (Article 26(5)).

    Data governance (Article 10)¶

    Vornik trains and fine-tunes no model. A system that uses models without training them falls under Article 10(6): only the testing data sets you use must meet Article 10's criteria, and they are yours. If you train or fine-tune a model, Article 10 applies to that work in full, outside Vornik. Vornik's project memory is input to each task, not a training, validation or testing data set; keeping input data relevant and representative falls to whoever controls it (Article 26(4)).

    Article 25(2): what else EaseIT Labs gives you¶

    • Technical documentation. The architecture and security documentation on this site, mapped to Annex IV above.
    • Known limitations and failure modes. The table under (b)(iii) and the security model's residual risks.
    • Technical access for testing and validation. A test instance and the evaluation tools on request.

    Enterprise customers can agree these in writing (Article 25(4)) as part of their contract; users of the free Community edition can ask at enterprise@vornik.io.
